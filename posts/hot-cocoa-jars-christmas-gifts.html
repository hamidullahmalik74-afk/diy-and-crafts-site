Hot Cocoa Jars: Easy Christmas Gift in 15 Minutes (Under $5 Each)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 27 Nov 2026 · Time: 15 minutes · Cost: under $5 each · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same cocoa jars you saw on Pinterest, layered in 15 minutes for under $5 each.

Hot cocoa jars solve the hardest Christmas gift problem: what do you give a teacher, a neighbor, or a coworker, that they will actually use, for under $5? You layer cocoa mix, chocolate chips, crushed candy cane, and mini marshmallows in a mason jar, tie on a tag with serving instructions, and you are done in 15 minutes. The layers look beautiful through the glass, and the gift is useful on a cold night.

This is a gift-in-a-jar, a proven Pinterest format, because the layers photograph well and the cost is tiny. One batch of supplies makes 4 to 6 jars, so you can make all your teacher and neighbor gifts in one afternoon. The jars keep for 2 to 3 months in a cool, dry place, so you can make them in November and give them in December.

How to pick your jars

Use 16-ounce mason jars, wide mouth if you can find them, because wide mouth jars are easier to layer and easier to spoon from later. Pint jars, 16 ounces, hold 2 to 3 servings, which is the right size for a gift. Half-pint jars, 8 ounces, hold 1 serving, and they are good for a small extra gift or a stocking stuffer. Quart jars, 32 ounces, are too big for one person and they look empty unless you fill them with a lot of mix.

Wash the jars and lids in hot soapy water, dry them fully, and check that the lids seal well. Any moisture inside the jar will make the cocoa mix clump, so the jars must be completely dry before you start. If you are reusing old jam jars, check that the lids are clean and do not smell of jam. A clean jar with a tight lid is the whole secret to a gift that keeps well.

What you need

  • Mason jars, 16 ounce, wide mouth, 4 to 6 jars — about $1 each, or $8 for a 12-pack
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder, 1 cup — about $3
  • Granulated sugar, 1 cup — about $1
  • Chocolate chips, 1 cup — about $2
  • Mini marshmallows, 1 cup — about $2
  • Crushed candy canes, 1/2 cup — about $2, or crush 4 candy canes yourself
  • Powdered milk, 1/2 cup, optional for a creamier mix — about $2
  • Ribbon or twine, for the tag — about $2
  • Card tags, for serving instructions — free from card scraps, or about $2 a pack

If you already have sugar, cocoa, and jars at home, your cost drops to about $2 per jar. Buy mini marshmallows, not regular ones, because regular marshmallows do not fit neatly in a layer. Buy candy canes after Christmas for next year if you want to save money, or crush them in a sealed bag with a rolling pin.

Steps

  1. Mix the cocoa base. In a large bowl, stir together 1 cup of unsweetened cocoa powder, 1 cup of granulated sugar, and 1/2 cup of powdered milk if you are using it. Stir well so there are no lumps. This is the cocoa mix layer, and it makes the base of the jar. Taste a spoonful mixed with hot milk later, and add a little more sugar if you like it sweeter.
  2. Layer the cocoa mix. Spoon the cocoa mix into the bottom of each jar, about 1/3 full. Tap the jar gently on the counter to level the layer, and wipe the inside of the glass with a dry cloth if any powder sticks to the sides. Clean layers are what make the jar look good in a photo and on a shelf.
  3. Add chocolate chips. Spoon a layer of chocolate chips on top of the cocoa mix, about 1/4 of the jar. Tap the jar again to level it. The chocolate chips add a darker layer, and they melt into the cocoa when the person makes it, for a richer drink.
  4. Add crushed candy cane. Sprinkle a thin layer of crushed candy cane on top of the chocolate chips. Do not make this layer too thick, because candy cane is very sweet and a little goes a long way. A thin red and white layer looks good and adds a mint flavor without overpowering the cocoa.
  5. Top with marshmallows. Fill the rest of the jar with mini marshmallows, pressing them down gently so they sit flat. The marshmallows are the top layer, and they are the first thing the person sees when they open the jar. Leave about 1/2 inch of space at the top so the lid closes without crushing them.
  6. Seal the jar. Wipe the rim of the jar clean, put the lid on, and tighten it. The jar should look like neat stripes: dark cocoa, chocolate chips, red and white candy cane, and white marshmallows on top. If a layer looks messy, start that jar again, it only takes a minute, and a clean jar is worth it.
  7. Make the tag. Write the serving instructions on a card tag: "Mix 3 tablespoons of cocoa mix with 1 cup of hot milk. Stir well. Top with marshmallows." Add the date you made it, and a short message like "Merry Christmas, from Hamid." Punch a hole in the tag, thread ribbon or twine through it, and tie it around the neck of the jar.
  8. Decorate and store. Tie a bow around the lid, or add a small sprig of pine or a cinnamon stick under the ribbon. Store the jars in a cool, dry place, away from sunlight, until you give them. They will keep for 2 to 3 months, so you can make them in November for December gifts.

Why cocoa jars clump or go stale

Clumping usually means moisture got into the jar. The jars must be fully dry before you layer, the lids must seal tightly, and the jars must be stored in a dry place, not in a damp kitchen or a cold garage. Stale cocoa usually means the jar was stored in sunlight or near a heater, which dries out the marshmallows and dulls the flavor. Keep the jars in a cool cupboard, and give them within 2 to 3 months. If a jar smells off or the marshmallows feel hard and sticky, do not give it, make a fresh one.

Cost breakdown

Jars $1 each, cocoa $3, sugar $1, chocolate chips $2, marshmallows $2, candy canes $2, tags and ribbon $2. Total about $18 for 4 jars if you buy everything new, and about $12 for 6 jars if you buy a 12-pack of jars and share the supplies. Each jar costs about $3 to $5, depending on what you already have at home. A similar cocoa gift set in a shop costs $10 to $15, so you save at least $5 per jar, and yours is handmade.

Tips to save time and money

Make the cocoa mix in a big batch, then layer all your jars in one sitting, like an assembly line. Buy jars in a 12-pack, they are cheaper per jar, and you will use the spares next year. Use candy canes you already have, or buy them on sale after Christmas for next year. Print the tags on your computer if you have a printer, or write them by hand for a personal touch. Make a test jar first, mix it with hot milk, and taste it, so you know the flavor is right before you make 6 jars.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use regular marshmallows, because they do not layer neatly and they crush the layers below. Do not make the candy cane layer too thick, because it makes the cocoa too sweet and too minty. Do not layer with wet hands or a wet spoon, because any moisture will clump the cocoa mix. Do not forget the tag, because without serving instructions the person will not know how much mix to use. Do not store the jars in the fridge, because condensation forms when you take them out, and that causes clumping.

Variations to try

Try a peppermint mocha jar: add 1 teaspoon of instant coffee to the cocoa mix layer. Try a white chocolate jar: use white chocolate chips instead of dark, and top with dried cranberries. Try a spicy cocoa jar: add 1/2 teaspoon of cinnamon and a pinch of chili powder to the cocoa mix. Try a kids' jar: use a small 8-ounce jar, skip the candy cane, and add colorful mini marshmallows on top. The same layering method also works for a cookie mix jar, with flour, sugar, and chocolate chips layered in the same way, and a tag with baking instructions.

Frequently asked questions

How many servings are in one jar? A 16-ounce jar holds about 2 to 3 servings, depending on how strong the person likes their cocoa. The tag says 3 tablespoons per cup of hot milk, so one jar makes about 3 cups.

Can I use hot water instead of milk? You can, but hot milk tastes much better, especially if you added powdered milk to the mix. Water makes a thinner cocoa, so use milk if you can, or use half milk and half water.

How long do cocoa jars keep? About 2 to 3 months in a cool, dry place, away from sunlight. The marshmallows will go hard first, so give the jars within a month if you can, and tell the person to use them soon.

Are cocoa jars safe for kids? Yes, if you use plain cocoa and skip the coffee. The candy cane layer is safe for most kids, but check for allergies, especially milk and nuts, and write the ingredients on the tag if you are giving the jar to someone you do not know well.
