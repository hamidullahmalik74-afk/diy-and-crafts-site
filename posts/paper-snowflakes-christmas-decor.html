Easy Paper Snowflakes: 5 Beautiful Designs (Free Templates)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 27 Nov 2026 · Time: 10 minutes each · Cost: $0 with paper at home · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same snowflakes you saw on Pinterest, folded and cut in 10 minutes each, with free templates.

Paper snowflakes are the cheapest Christmas decoration you can make. You need white paper and scissors, and nothing else to start. Fold the paper, cut a few shapes, open it up, and you have a snowflake that looks different every time. Each one takes about 10 minutes, and a set of 5 makes a window, a garland, or a gift tag look finished.

This is a good project for kids, for a classroom, and for a first paper craft. The folding is simple, the cutting is forgiving, and a mistake costs one sheet of paper. The templates below give you 5 designs to start with: classic, lacy, star, tree, and heart. Once you learn the fold, you can make your own designs too.

How to fold a snowflake

Start with a square of paper. If you only have A4 or letter paper, fold one corner to the opposite edge, crease it, and cut off the strip at the bottom, so you are left with a square. Fold the square in half into a triangle, then fold it in half again into a smaller triangle. Fold it in thirds, like a fan, so you have a narrow wedge with a pointed tip at the bottom and a straight edge at the top. This is the fold for a 6-point snowflake, the classic shape.

Crease every fold sharply with your fingernail or a ruler, because sharp creases make clean cuts and a neat snowflake. Keep the folded wedge with the point at the bottom and the open edges at the top. Cut shapes out of the sides, small triangles, curves, and notches, but do not cut all the way across the wedge, or the snowflake will fall apart. Open it up slowly, flatten it under a heavy book for a few minutes, and you will see the design.

What you need

  • White paper, A4 or letter, or white coffee filters — free if you have paper at home, about $3 for a pack
  • Sharp scissors, small ones are best — you have these at home
  • A pencil, to draw the template lines — free
  • A ruler, for sharp creases — free
  • Clear tape or sticky tack, to hang them — about $2
  • Twine or thread, for a garland — about $2, or use what you have
  • Glitter or gold paint, optional — about $3 if you want a sparkly finish

If you already have white paper and scissors at home, this project costs $0. Use plain white copy paper for practice, and use slightly thicker white card for snowflakes you want to keep. Coffee filters make beautiful snowflakes too, because they are already round, thin, and easy to cut. Do not use colored paper for your first snowflake, white shows the design best on a dark window.

The 5 designs

  1. Classic snowflake. Fold the paper into a 6-point wedge. Cut small triangles out of both sides, 3 on each side, and cut a small V shape out of the top edge. Open it up. This is the simplest design, with 6 points and a clean, even pattern. It is the best one to start with, and it looks good on a window or a gift tag. If the points look uneven, refold it and trim the top edge straight.
  2. Lacy snowflake. Fold the paper into a 6-point wedge. Cut many small shapes: tiny triangles, half-circles, and thin slits, all along both sides. Leave more paper than you cut away, so the snowflake stays strong. Open it up slowly, because lacy snowflakes are delicate. This design looks like real lace, and it is beautiful on a dark background or a Christmas card. Use small scissors and take your time.
  3. Star snowflake. Fold the paper into a 6-point wedge. Cut a deep V shape out of the top edge, so the top of each point becomes a sharp star tip. Cut a small diamond out of the middle of each side. Open it up. The snowflake will have 6 sharp points, like a star, with a diamond in each arm. This is a good design for a garland, because the points show up well from a distance.
  4. Tree snowflake. Fold the paper into a 6-point wedge. Draw a small tree shape on the wedge with a pencil, with the trunk at the pointed tip and the branches at the top edge. Cut around the tree shape, leaving the trunk attached to the tip. Open it up. You will see 6 small trees around a center star. This is a fun design for kids, because they can see the tree shape appear when they open the paper.
  5. Heart snowflake. Fold the paper into a 6-point wedge. Cut a small heart shape out of the top edge, and cut a small heart out of the middle of each side. Open it up. The snowflake will have hearts around the edge and in the center. This design is good for a gift tag or a card, because it is pretty and a little different from a normal snowflake. Use a sharp pencil to draw the heart first, then cut along the line.

Free templates

The templates are the cutting guides for the 5 designs above. Draw the lines on your folded wedge with a pencil before you cut, and follow the lines with your scissors. For the classic design, draw 3 small triangles on each side. For the lacy design, draw tiny triangles and half-circles. For the star design, draw a deep V at the top. For the tree design, draw a small tree. For the heart design, draw a small heart. You do not need to print anything, just draw the shapes on the folded paper. If you want a printable version, draw the wedge shape on a sheet of paper, mark the cutting lines, and photocopy it for a classroom or a group.

How to use your snowflakes

Tape them to a window with clear tape or sticky tack, in a group of 5, with different sizes. String them on twine to make a garland, by taping the top point of each snowflake to the twine, spaced 6 inches apart. Tape one to the front of a wrapped gift as a decoration, or glue one to a Christmas card. Hang them from the ceiling with thread, at different heights, so they look like falling snow. Iron them flat first if they curl, on a low heat with a cloth over the top, so they lie flat on a window or a card.

Cost breakdown

White paper $3 for a pack, scissors you already have, tape $2, twine $2. Total about $7 if you buy everything new, and $0 if you already have paper, scissors, and tape at home. One pack of paper makes 20 to 30 snowflakes, so each snowflake costs about 10 cents. A pack of paper snowflake decorations in a shop costs $5 to $10 for 10 snowflakes, so you save a lot, and yours are handmade and different every time.

Tips to save time and money

Make a batch of snowflakes in one sitting, with the same fold, and cut different designs from the same fold. Use coffee filters for quick snowflakes, because they are already round and you skip the square step. Let kids draw the cutting lines with a pencil, then cut along the lines, so they learn the design without guessing. Flatten finished snowflakes under a heavy book overnight, so they lie flat on a window. Save the best snowflakes in a folder between sheets of paper, so you can use them again next year.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not cut all the way across the folded wedge, because the snowflake will fall apart into pieces. Leave at least 1/4 inch of paper uncut at the fold. Do not use dull scissors, because they tear the paper instead of cutting it, and the edges look rough. Do not open the snowflake too fast, because lacy snowflakes can tear, open it slowly and flatten it gently. Do not use paper that is too thick for your first snowflake, thick card is hard to fold and hard to cut, start with thin copy paper.

Variations to try

Try a colored snowflake with blue or silver paper, for a different look on a white wall. Try a large snowflake from a big sheet of paper, for a window display, or a tiny snowflake from a small square, for a gift tag. Try a glitter snowflake, by brushing the finished snowflake with glue and sprinkling glitter on it, then letting it dry. Try a 3D snowflake, by folding 6 snowflakes in half and gluing them together at the center, so it stands up on a table. The same folding method also makes paper stars and paper flowers, just change the cutting shapes.

Frequently asked questions

What paper is best for snowflakes? Plain white copy paper is best for practice, because it is thin, cheap, and easy to fold and cut. White card is better for snowflakes you want to keep, because it is stronger and lies flatter. Coffee filters are easiest, because they are already round.

Can kids make these? Yes. The folding is easy, and the cutting is safe with child scissors. An adult should help with the folding the first time, and with sharp scissors for small children. Kids love opening the paper to see the design appear.

How do I make a snowflake garland? Make 5 to 7 snowflakes in different sizes, tape the top point of each one to a length of twine, spaced 6 inches apart, and hang the twine across a window or a mantel. Use clear tape on the back, so it does not show from the front.

Why did my snowflake fall apart? You cut all the way across the folded wedge, or you cut too much paper away. Leave at least 1/4 inch uncut at the fold, and cut small shapes, not big ones, until you learn how much paper you can remove.
