7 DIY Christmas Gift Wrap Ideas (Kraft Paper + $0 Supplies)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 27 Nov 2026 · Time: 5 to 10 minutes per gift · Cost: $0 with supplies at home · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same wrapped gifts you saw on Pinterest, made with kraft paper and things you already have.

Good gift wrap does not need expensive paper. A roll of plain kraft paper, some twine, and a few things from your kitchen and garden will make a gift look handmade in 5 to 10 minutes. These 7 ideas all start with kraft paper, because it is cheap, strong, and it suits every decoration you add to it. One roll costs about $5 and wraps 10 to 15 gifts.

People are wrapping gifts right now, and they want a quick upgrade, not a shopping trip. Each idea below uses supplies you already have: yarn, stamps, dried orange, cinnamon, fabric scraps, and greenery from outside. Pick one idea for all your gifts for a matching set, or mix them for a handmade look under the tree.

What you need

  • Kraft paper roll, brown — about $5 for a large roll, wraps 10 to 15 gifts
  • Twine, ribbon, or yarn — about $2 to $3, or use what you have at home
  • Scissors and tape — you have these at home
  • Potato or cork, for stamping — free from the kitchen
  • Green or red acrylic paint, for stamps — about $3 if you do not have any
  • Dried orange slices — about $4 a bag, or dry your own
  • Cinnamon sticks — about $2 a bag
  • Fabric scraps, for wrapping and bows — free from old clothes or sheets
  • Small evergreen sprigs, pinecones, and rosemary — free from outside

If you already have kraft paper, twine, and paint at home, these ideas cost $0 extra. Buy kraft paper in a plain brown roll, not a printed one, because the plain paper is the background for every decoration. Keep the tape on the back of the gift, so the front stays clean and the decoration is the first thing people see.

The 7 ideas

  1. Stamped trees. Cut a potato in half and carve a simple triangle tree shape into the cut side with a knife. Dab it in green paint, stamp it in a row across the kraft paper, and let it dry for 10 minutes. Add a small star on top with a gold marker. This is the fastest idea, and kids can help with the stamping. Wrap the gift after the paint dries, or stamp the paper first and wrap later.
  2. Yarn tree. Wrap the gift in kraft paper and tie twine around it once. Cut a triangle from green yarn, or wind yarn back and forth into a tree shape on the front of the gift, and tape the ends on the back. Add a tiny star cut from gold card. This looks like a hand-drawn tree, and it takes about 5 minutes. Use red yarn for a different look, or white yarn on dark paper.
  3. Dried orange and cinnamon. Wrap the gift in kraft paper and tie it with twine. Tuck a dried orange slice and a cinnamon stick under the twine on the front. Add a small sprig of rosemary or pine for green. This is the classic Christmas look, it smells good, and it takes 2 minutes once the gift is wrapped. Dry your own orange slices at 200 degrees Fahrenheit for 2 to 3 hours if you do not want to buy them.
  4. Evergreen sprig. Wrap the gift in kraft paper and tie it with ribbon or twine. Tuck a small evergreen sprig, a pinecone, and a piece of ribbon under the tie. This is the simplest idea, and it works best on small gifts. Cut the sprig short, about 3 inches, so it does not snap off when the gift is carried. Mist the sprig with water if the gift will sit under the tree for a few days.
  5. Fabric wrap. Wrap the gift in a square of fabric instead of paper, using the furoshiki method: put the gift in the middle, bring opposite corners together, tie them in a knot on top, and tuck the other two corners in. Use an old scarf, a tea towel, or a piece of cotton fabric. The fabric is part of the gift, so there is no waste, and it works well for odd shapes like books and bottles. Iron the fabric first so the folds look clean.
  6. Hand-lettered tag. Wrap the gift in plain kraft paper and tie it with twine. Cut a tag from card, write the person's name in large letters with a black marker, and add a small drawn tree or star. A hand-lettered tag makes even the simplest wrap look thoughtful, and it takes 2 minutes. If your handwriting is shaky, write in capital letters, they are easier to make neat. Tie the tag on with the same twine.
  7. Paper snowflakes. Cut a small paper snowflake from white paper, fold and cut it like the snowflakes in our other post, and tape it to the front of a kraft-wrapped gift. Add a ribbon tie and a small tag. This is perfect for kids' gifts, because the snowflake is the decoration and the gift inside is a surprise. Make a batch of snowflakes in one sitting, then use them on all your gifts.

How to wrap neatly

Cut the paper so it overlaps by about 1 inch on the side, and by 2 inches on the ends. Fold the side edges in first, then fold the ends into neat triangles, like an envelope, and tape them flat. Crease every fold with your fingernail or a ruler, because sharp creases make the wrap look tidy. Put the tape on the bottom or the back, not on the front, and use double-sided tape if you want no tape to show at all. If the gift is an odd shape, wrap it in tissue paper first, then in kraft paper, so the corners do not poke through.

Cost breakdown

Kraft paper $5, twine $3, paint $3, dried oranges $4, cinnamon $2. Total about $17 if you buy everything new, and $0 to $5 if you already have paper, twine, and paint at home. One roll of kraft paper wraps 10 to 15 gifts, so each gift costs about 50 cents in paper. A roll of printed wrapping paper costs about the same, but it does not look handmade, and you still need ribbon and tags.

Tips to save time and money

Wrap all your gifts in one sitting, with the same paper and the same tie, then add the decoration last. Cut all your tags at once, stamp all your paper at once, and dry all your orange slices at once. Use one decoration per gift, not three, because a single dried orange or a single sprig looks cleaner than a crowded front. Keep a small box of wrapping supplies, twine, ribbon, tags, and dried oranges, so you can wrap a last-minute gift in 5 minutes. Save fabric scraps and ribbon from last year, they work just as well this year.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use wet paint or wet greenery on a wrapped gift, because it will stain the paper. Let stamped paper dry fully before you wrap, and let evergreen sprigs dry after misting. Do not tie the twine too tight, because it will dent a soft gift like clothing. Do not use too much tape on the front, because it shows in photos and it tears the paper when the gift is opened. Do not mix too many colors on one gift, pick two, kraft brown plus green, or kraft brown plus red, and keep it simple.

Variations to try

Try white kraft paper with red twine for a snowy look, or black paper with gold ribbon for an elegant look. Try a brown paper bag as a gift bag, folded at the top and tied with twine, for a small gift. Try a newspaper wrap with a red ribbon for a vintage look, or a child's drawing as wrapping paper for a grandparent's gift. The same 7 ideas work on birthday gifts too, just change the colors and the tag. A dried orange and cinnamon gift works for a teacher, a neighbor, or a friend, and it costs almost nothing.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I buy kraft paper? At a craft store, a dollar store, or online, in a large roll. A plain brown roll is cheapest, and one roll wraps 10 to 15 gifts. You can also use a brown paper grocery bag, cut open and ironed flat.

Can I use these ideas on any gift? Yes. They work on boxes, books, bottles, and soft gifts. For a soft gift, put it in a box first, then wrap the box, so the shape is neat and the twine does not dent it.

How do I dry orange slices? Slice an orange thin, about 1/4 inch, put the slices on a baking sheet lined with parchment, and bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit for 2 to 3 hours, turning them once. Let them cool fully, then store them in a dry jar. They will keep for months.

Will the greenery wilt under the tree? A little, after a few days. Cut the sprig short, mist it with water, and add it the day before the gift is given if you can. A dried orange and cinnamon decoration will not wilt at all, so use that for gifts that will sit under the tree for a week.
