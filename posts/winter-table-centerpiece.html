DIY Winter Table Centerpiece with Pinecones and Candles (Under $15, 30 Minutes)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 28 Nov 2026 · Time: 30 minutes · Cost: under $15 · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same centerpiece you saw on Pinterest, made in 30 minutes for under $15.

A winter table centerpiece makes an ordinary dinner feel special, and this one takes 30 minutes and costs under $15. You arrange three candles in glass holders on a wooden tray, fill the gaps with evergreen sprigs, pinecones, and dried oranges, and you are done. No gluing, no wiring, no special tools, and you can take it apart and reuse everything next year.

This is a good project for a Christmas dinner table, for a winter party, and for a cozy home. The centerpiece is low enough that people can talk across the table, and it is easy to move if you need the space. A similar centerpiece in a shop often costs $35 to $60, so you save a lot, and yours uses real pinecones and greenery you can find outside. The candles give a warm light, and the evergreen gives a fresh scent, without any work at all.

How a winter centerpiece works

A good centerpiece has three parts: a base, a height, and a filler. The base is a wooden tray, a cutting board, or a long platter, about 18 to 24 inches long, so it fits the table without crowding the plates. The height comes from three candles in glass holders, placed at different heights, so the eye moves along the table. The filler is evergreen sprigs, pinecones, dried oranges, and cinnamon sticks, tucked in around the candles to hide the tray and make it look full. Keep the candles in glass, and keep the greenery away from the flame, so the centerpiece is safe as well as pretty.

Use real evergreen if you can, because it smells fresh and looks natural. Cut sprigs from a fir, pine, or cedar tree, or buy a bunch at a garden store for about $3. Pinecones are free if you collect them outside, or about $3 for a bag at a craft store. Dried oranges take a few hours to make, so buy them ready-made if you are short on time, or make them the day before.

What you need

  • A wooden tray, cutting board, or long platter, 18 to 24 inches — about $5 at a thrift store, or use one you have
  • Three candles in glass holders, pillar or jar candles — about $3 to $6 total
  • Evergreen sprigs, fir, pine, or cedar — about $3, or free from your garden
  • Pinecones, 6 to 8, small and medium — free outside, or about $3 for a bag
  • Dried orange slices, 3 to 4 — about $2, or make them at home
  • Cinnamon sticks, 4 to 6 — about $1
  • Small red berries or cranberries, fresh or fake — about $2
  • A ribbon, to tie around the tray — about $1, optional

Choose candles that are all the same colour, white or cream, so the centerpiece looks calm. Use glass holders, not open candles, because the glass keeps the flame away from the greenery. If you use fake evergreen, choose a good quality one, because cheap plastic greenery looks flat in photos and on the table.

Steps

  1. Place the tray. Put the wooden tray in the centre of the table, lengthways. Leave at least 12 inches of table on each side for plates and glasses, so the centerpiece does not crowd the table. If the table is small, use a shorter tray, about 12 inches long, and use two candles instead of three.
  2. Set the candles. Place the three glass candle holders on the tray, one in the middle and one at each end, or tallest in the middle and shorter at the sides. Leave about 4 inches between each candle and the edge of the tray, so the greenery has room to fill in.
  3. Add the evergreen. Lay evergreen sprigs flat on the tray, around the base of the candles, with the tips pointing outwards. Overlap the sprigs so you cannot see the tray underneath. Trim the sprigs with scissors if they stick out too far.
  4. Add the pinecones. Tuck 6 to 8 pinecones into the gaps between the candles, on top of the evergreen. Use small pinecones near the candles and larger ones at the ends, so the centerpiece looks balanced. Turn the pinecones so their prettiest side faces out.
  5. Add the oranges and cinnamon. Tuck dried orange slices upright between the pinecones, so they stand up and catch the light. Lay cinnamon sticks in small bundles of three, tied with twine, and tuck them into the evergreen. Add a few red berries or cranberries for colour.
  6. Check the safety. Make sure no greenery, pinecone, or ribbon touches a candle flame, and that the candles are at least 2 inches away from anything that can burn. Remove the ribbon and greenery before you light the candles, or keep the flames small and never leave them unattended.
  7. Finish and light. Step back and look at the centerpiece from across the table. Fill any bare spots with a sprig or a pinecone, and trim anything that looks messy. Light the candles just before dinner, and enjoy the warm light.

Why a centerpiece looks bare or messy

A bare centerpiece usually means the tray is too big for the filler, or the greenery is too thin. Use more evergreen than you think you need, and overlap the sprigs so you cannot see the tray. A messy centerpiece usually means the candles are different heights and colours, or the pinecones are all the same size. Use candles in the same colour, vary their heights, and mix small and large pinecones. If the centerpiece looks lopsided, move the tallest candle to the centre, and put the same number of pinecones on each side.

Cost breakdown

Tray $5, candles $5, evergreen $3, pinecones $3, dried oranges $2, cinnamon $1, berries $2. Total about $21 if you buy everything new, and under $15 if you use a tray and candles you already have, and collect pinecones and evergreen for free. A similar centerpiece in a shop costs $35 to $60, so you save at least $20, and you can reuse the tray, candles, and pinecones next year.

Tips to save time and money

Collect pinecones and evergreen from your garden or a park, and wash them in warm soapy water before you use them. Buy candles and trays at a thrift store, because they cost a few dollars there and look the same on the table. Make dried oranges the day before: slice oranges thin, bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit for 2 to 3 hours, and let them cool. Use the same tray and candles for a New Year table, just swap the Christmas berries for white candles and silver pinecones. Store the pinecones and dried oranges in a dry box, so they last for next winter.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not put greenery, pinecones, or ribbon near a candle flame, because dried greenery catches fire easily. Do not use a tray that is too wide for your table, because guests will not have room for their plates. Do not use scented candles with a strong smell, because they can overpower the food. Do not make the centerpiece too tall, because people need to see each other across the table. Do not leave candles burning unattended, and blow them out when you leave the table.

Variations to try

Try a white winter version: use white candles, white pinecones dusted with fake snow, and silver berries, for a snowy table. Try a rustic version: use a wooden slice as the base, burlap ribbon, and cinnamon sticks, for a farmhouse table. Try a small version for a coffee table: use a round tray, one candle, and a few pinecones, for a cozy living room. Try a floating candle version: use tall glass cylinders with water, cranberries, and a floating candle, for a table that needs a little more light.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a winter centerpiece last? The evergreen lasts about 1 to 2 weeks if you mist it with water every few days. The pinecones, dried oranges, and candles last for years if you store them dry. Replace the evergreen when it dries out and starts to drop needles.

Can I use fake evergreen? Yes, fake evergreen lasts for years and looks good in photos. Real evergreen smells better and looks more natural, but it dries out. Choose real evergreen for a special dinner, and fake evergreen for a centerpiece you want to keep up all winter.

Is it safe to put candles near pinecones? Yes, if the candles are in glass holders and the pinecones are at least 2 inches away from the flame. Never let greenery or pinecones touch a flame, and never leave burning candles unattended.

What size tray should I use? A tray 18 to 24 inches long fits a table for 6 to 8 people. For a table for 4, use a tray 12 to 15 inches long, with two candles. Measure your table first, and leave at least 12 inches on each side for plates.
