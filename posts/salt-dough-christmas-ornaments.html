Salt Dough Christmas Ornaments (3 Ingredients, Under $5)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 27 Nov 2026 · Time: 1 hour plus baking · Cost: under $5 · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same ornaments you saw on Pinterest, made with 3 ingredients you already have.

Salt dough ornaments are the cheapest Christmas craft I know. You need flour, salt, and water, and nothing else to start. Mix them into a soft dough, cut out stars and trees, bake them until hard, and paint them. The whole batch costs under $5, and one batch makes about 12 to 15 ornaments, enough for your tree and a few gifts.

This is a good project for kids, for a first Christmas craft, and for a keepsake gift. A handprint ornament made this year is still on a grandparent's tree years later. The dough is safe to handle, the steps are simple, and a mistake costs almost nothing, because you can roll the dough out again and cut it one more time.

How salt dough works

Salt dough hardens in the oven because the salt draws out moisture and the flour sets firm as it dries. The basic ratio is 2 cups of plain flour, 1 cup of fine table salt, and about 3/4 cup of warm water. Stir the flour and salt together first, then add the water a little at a time. You want a soft dough that holds together without sticking to your hands. If it sticks, add a spoonful of flour. If it crumbles, add a spoonful of water.

Knead the dough on a floured surface for 3 to 4 minutes, until it feels smooth. Let it rest for 10 minutes under a clean cloth while you get the cutters ready. Resting makes the dough easier to roll and less likely to shrink in the oven. Roll it to about 1/4 inch thick. Thinner ornaments dry faster and hang better, but ornaments thinner than 1/8 inch can crack, so keep them even and not too thin.

What you need

  • Plain flour, 2 cups — about $1, you probably have it at home
  • Fine table salt, 1 cup — about $0.50
  • Warm water, about 3/4 cup — free
  • Christmas cookie cutters, stars, trees, snowmen — about $5 for a set, or use a glass rim
  • A straw or skewer, to make the hanging hole — free
  • Acrylic paint and a small brush — about $3 to $5 if you do not have any
  • Clear varnish or Mod Podge, to seal — about $4
  • Ribbon or twine, for hanging — about $2

If you already have flour, salt, paint, and ribbon at home, this project costs almost nothing. Buy fine salt, not coarse salt, because coarse salt leaves a rough surface. Use plain flour, not self-raising flour, because self-raising flour puffs up in the oven and the shapes lose their edges.

Steps

  1. Mix the dough. Stir 2 cups of flour and 1 cup of fine salt in a large bowl. Add 3/4 cup of warm water a little at a time, stirring as you go. When it starts to clump, tip it onto a floured surface and knead for 3 to 4 minutes until smooth. Cover and rest for 10 minutes.
  2. Roll and cut. Roll the dough to 1/4 inch thick, turning it once so it stays even. Press the cutters straight down, lift them straight up, and lift each shape with a flat spatula. Gather the scraps, roll them again, and cut more shapes. You should get 12 to 15 ornaments from one batch.
  3. Make the hole. Before baking, push a straw or skewer through the top of each shape, about 1/4 inch from the edge. Twist it gently as you pull it out, so the hole stays round and clean. If you forget this step, you can drill a small hole after baking, but it is much easier now.
  4. Add a handprint or pattern. For a keepsake, press a child's hand gently into a round shape before baking. For a plain ornament, press a stamp, a fork, or a piece of lace lightly into the surface. Keep the marks shallow so the shape does not tear.
  5. Bake low and slow. Put the shapes on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit (about 95 degrees Celsius) for 2 to 3 hours, turning them once halfway through. They are done when they feel hard and dry all the way through. Do not rush this with a hot oven, because high heat puffs and cracks the dough.
  6. Cool completely. Leave the ornaments on the tray until they are fully cool, at least 30 minutes. Warm dough feels soft in the middle, so do not judge it until it cools. If an ornament still feels soft underneath, put it back in the oven for another 30 minutes.
  7. Paint and seal. Paint with acrylic paint, two thin coats, and let each coat dry for 20 minutes. Add names, dates, or dots with a fine brush. When the paint is dry, seal both sides with clear varnish or Mod Podge. Sealing matters, because unsealed salt dough absorbs moisture from the air and can go soft over time.
  8. Hang them. Thread ribbon or twine through the hole and tie a loop. Hang them on the tree, tie one to a gift, or give a set of three in a small box as a teacher gift.

Why salt dough ornaments crack

Cracking usually means the oven was too hot, the dough was rolled unevenly, or the ornament dried too fast. Bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit and no higher, keep the thickness even, and let them cool slowly in the oven with the door closed for 10 minutes after you turn the heat off. Air bubbles are another cause. Knead the dough well and press out air pockets before you roll it. If a small crack appears, fill it with a little fresh dough before baking, or with white paint after, and seal it well.

Cost breakdown

Flour $1, salt $0.50, paint $4, varnish $4, ribbon $2. Total about $11.50 if you buy everything new, and under $5 if you already have paint, varnish, and ribbon at home. One batch makes 12 to 15 ornaments, so each ornament costs about 30 to 40 cents. A set of similar ornaments in a shop often costs $8 to $15, so you save a lot, and yours are handmade.

Tips to save time and money

Make the dough the night before and keep it wrapped in plastic in the fridge, then roll and cut the next day. Use cookie cutters you already own, or cut simple shapes with a knife: a star, a tree, and a circle are all easy freehand. Paint with leftover acrylic paint from other projects. Write the year on the back of each ornament with a fine marker, so you can remember when it was made. Store finished ornaments wrapped in tissue paper in a dry box, not in a damp garage or basement.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use self-raising flour, because it puffs up and ruins the shape. Do not bake at a high temperature to save time, because the outside hardens while the inside stays soft, and the ornament cracks later. Do not skip the sealing step, because unsealed ornaments absorb humidity and soften. Do not paint before the ornament is fully dry and cool, because the paint traps moisture inside. Do not make the hanging hole too close to the edge, because the dough can break there when you hang it.

Variations to try

Try a cinnamon version: replace 1/2 cup of flour with ground cinnamon for brown ornaments that smell like Christmas. Try a handprint ornament for a baby's first Christmas, with the name and year on the back. Try white ornaments with gold paint on the edges for a simple, elegant tree. Try a set of gift tags: cut small circles, stamp a letter on each one, and tie them to presents. The same dough also makes a small star garland, just make the holes on both sides and thread them on twine.

Frequently asked questions

How long do salt dough ornaments last? Years, if they are fully baked, painted, and sealed on both sides, and stored dry. Keep them out of damp rooms and direct sunlight, and they will last for many Christmases.

Can kids help make them? Yes. Mixing, kneading, cutting, and painting are all kid-friendly. An adult should handle the oven. Keep the dough away from pets, because the salt is not safe for them to eat.

Can I air-dry them instead of baking? You can, but it takes 2 to 3 days, and thick ornaments can stay soft inside. Baking at a low temperature is faster and more reliable, and it dries the ornament all the way through.

Why did my ornaments puff up? The oven was too hot, or the flour was self-raising. Use plain flour, bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit, and roll the dough evenly to 1/4 inch.
