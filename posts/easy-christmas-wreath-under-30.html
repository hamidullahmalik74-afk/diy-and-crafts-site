Easy Christmas Wreath DIY (Under $30, 30 Minutes)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 27 Nov 2026 · Time: 30 minutes · Cost: under $30 · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same wreath you saw on Pinterest, made in 30 minutes for under $30.

A Christmas wreath is the fastest way to make a front door look finished. This one takes 30 minutes, costs under $30, and needs no florist skills. You wrap evergreen sprigs around a wire form, tie them in place, and add dried oranges, cinnamon sticks, and a big bow. The result looks full and expensive, and a similar wreath in a shop often costs $50 to $80.

Use real evergreen if you can, because it smells like Christmas and looks natural. If you cannot get fresh greenery, use a cheap faux garland from a craft store and the same steps still work. The key is layering: small bunches, all pointing the same way, overlapping each other, until you cannot see the wire underneath.

How to pick your greenery

Look for sprigs that are flexible, not brittle. Pine, fir, cedar, and eucalyptus all work, and mixing two types looks better than one. Pine gives you long needles and a full shape, cedar gives you soft, flat sprays, and eucalyptus adds a pale green color. Ask a Christmas tree lot for offcuts, because many lots give away trimmings for free, or sell a bundle for a few dollars. That is the cheapest way to get fresh greenery.

Cut your greenery into small bunches, about 6 inches long, with 3 to 4 sprigs in each bunch. Strip the bottom inch of needles from each bunch so you have a clean stem to wire down. Make 10 to 12 bunches before you start, so you are not stopping to cut more in the middle. If you are using faux garland, cut it into the same 6-inch pieces instead.

What you need

  • Wire wreath form, 12 or 14 inch — about $4 to $6 at a craft store
  • Fresh evergreen sprigs, 2 to 3 bunches — about $8 to $12, or free tree trimmings
  • Floral wire, 22 gauge green — about $3
  • Dried orange slices, 5 to 6 — about $4, or dry your own in the oven
  • Cinnamon sticks, 5 to 6 — about $2
  • Pinecones, 3 to 4 small ones — free from outside, or about $3 a bag
  • Wide ribbon, 2 inch, for a bow — about $4
  • Wire cutters and scissors — you probably have these at home

If you already have ribbon, wire, and pinecones at home, your total can drop to about $15. Buy the wire form in the size that suits your door: 12 inches is good for a small door or a wall, 14 inches is better for a standard front door. A larger form needs more greenery, so do not buy a huge form for your first wreath.

Steps

  1. Make the bunches. Cut your greenery into 6-inch bunches of 3 to 4 sprigs. Strip the bottom inch of needles. Lay the bunches out in a row so you can grab them quickly. Make 10 to 12 bunches, and keep a few spare sprigs for filling gaps later.
  2. Wire the first bunch. Lay the first bunch on the wire form, with the stems pointing clockwise. Wrap floral wire around the stems and the form 3 times, pull it tight, and do not cut the wire yet. Keep the wire attached to the spool, so you can keep wrapping as you go.
  3. Layer the next bunch. Lay the next bunch over the stems of the first one, so the greenery covers the wire. Wrap the wire around it 3 times, tight. Keep going around the form, always in the same direction, overlapping each bunch by about half. The wreath will start to look full after 4 or 5 bunches.
  4. Finish the circle. When you reach the start, tuck the last bunch under the first bunch, so the stems are hidden. Wrap the wire a few more times, twist the end around the form, and cut it. Fluff the greenery with your hands, pulling some sprigs out and pushing others in, until the circle looks even all the way around.
  5. Add dried oranges. Push a short piece of floral wire through the top of each dried orange slice, twist it into a small loop at the back, and wire it to the form. Space 5 or 6 slices evenly around the wreath. Dried oranges add a warm color and a light citrus smell, and they are the look in most 2026 holiday roundups.
  6. Add cinnamon and pinecones. Tie 2 cinnamon sticks together with a short piece of wire, then wire the bundle to the wreath. Do this in 2 or 3 spots. Wire the pinecones the same way, tucking them into gaps where the greenery is thin. Keep the decorations on the outer half of the wreath, so the inner hole stays clean.
  7. Make and attach the bow. Make a simple loop bow from wide ribbon: fold it into 2 loops on each side, pinch the middle, and wrap a short piece of wire around the center. Wire the bow to the bottom or the top of the wreath. Let the tails hang down about 6 inches and cut them into a V shape.
  8. Hang it. Hang the wreath on a nail, a wreath hanger, or a ribbon loop over the door. Step back and look at it from a few feet away. Fill any thin spots with spare sprigs, tucked in and wired down. Mist fresh greenery with water every few days to keep it looking fresh.

Why a wreath looks thin or flat

A thin wreath usually means the bunches were too small, too far apart, or all pointing in different directions. Use bunches of 3 to 4 sprigs, overlap each bunch by half, and keep them all pointing the same way around the circle. A flat wreath usually means the greenery was pressed down too hard while wiring. Fluff it after wiring, pulling the outer sprigs outward and the inner sprigs inward, so it has depth. If you can still see wire, add a small bunch in that spot, it takes 30 seconds.

Cost breakdown

Wire form $5, greenery $10, floral wire $3, dried oranges $4, cinnamon $2, ribbon $4. Total about $28 if you buy everything new, and about $15 if you use free tree trimmings and ribbon you already have. A similar full wreath in a shop costs $50 to $80, so you save at least $20, and yours is fresh and handmade.

Tips to save time and money

Ask a Christmas tree lot for free trimmings, or cut greenery from your own garden if you have pine or cedar. Buy dried orange slices in the craft aisle, or dry your own: slice an orange thin, bake at 200 degrees Fahrenheit for 2 to 3 hours, and let it cool. Make the bow from ribbon you already own, and keep the wreath simple the first time, greenery, oranges, and a bow are enough. Store the wire form after Christmas, because you can reuse it next year.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not wire the bunches in different directions, because the wreath will look messy and the stems will show. Do not use a form that is too big for the greenery you have, because it will look thin no matter how much you fluff it. Do not hang a fresh wreath in direct sun or near a heater, because it will dry out fast. Do not skip the wire, glue alone will not hold fresh greenery on a door that opens and closes. Do not overload one side with decorations, because the wreath will hang crooked.

Variations to try

Try an all-green wreath with no fruit, just eucalyptus and a cream bow, for a calm, modern look. Try a red and gold wreath with small baubles wired in among the greenery. Try a small 8-inch wreath for a window or a mirror, using the same steps with fewer bunches. Try a faux wreath with a garland from a craft store, so it lasts for years and you can store it flat. The same layering method also works for a table centerpiece, just use a flat wire form and add candles in the middle.

Frequently asked questions

How long will a fresh wreath last? About 3 to 4 weeks outdoors in cool weather, if you mist it with water every few days. Indoors, or in warm sun, it will dry faster, so hang it outside if you can.

Can I make this with fake greenery? Yes. Use a faux garland, cut it into 6-inch pieces, and follow the same steps. Faux greenery is easier to wire, it does not drop needles, and you can reuse the wreath next year.

What if I cannot find a wire form? Make a circle from a wire coat hanger, bent into a ring, or use a foam wreath form from a craft store. The layering steps are the same, just wrap the wire a little tighter on a foam form.

How do I keep it from drying out? Mist it with water every 2 to 3 days, keep it out of direct sun and away from heaters, and hang it on a shaded door if you can. A little needle drop is normal with fresh greenery.
