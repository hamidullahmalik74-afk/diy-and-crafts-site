Easy Soy Candle in a Mason Jar (Under $10, 45 Minutes)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 28 Nov 2026 · Time: 45 minutes plus setting · Cost: under $10 · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same candle you saw on Pinterest, poured at home for under $10.

A homemade soy candle is one of the easiest winter crafts, and it looks like it came from a boutique shop. You melt soy wax, stir in a Christmas scent, pour it into a mason jar, and let it set. The whole project takes about 45 minutes of active work, plus overnight to harden, and one candle costs under $10. A similar candle in a shop often costs $18 to $28, so you save a lot, and yours is handmade.

This is a good project for a first candle, for a cozy home, and for a handmade gift. A mason jar candle suits a teacher, a neighbour, or a Secret Santa, and you can make a batch of four in one afternoon. Soy wax is easy to work with, it burns cleanly, and it holds scent well, which is why most homemade candles use it. The jar is reusable too, so the gift keeps going after the candle is finished.

How soy candle making works

Soy wax melts at a low temperature, about 120 to 180 degrees Fahrenheit, depending on the brand. You melt it in a double boiler or a pouring jug set in hot water, never directly over a flame. Once it is fully melted, you take it off the heat and stir in fragrance oil for about two minutes, so the scent binds to the wax. Then you pour it slowly into a jar with a wick already fixed in the centre, and leave it alone to harden. Moving the jar while it sets causes sinkholes and a rough top, so patience is part of the craft.

The wick matters more than most beginners expect. A cotton wick with a metal tab, sized for the jar, gives a steady flame. A wick that is too small tunnels down the middle and leaves wax on the sides. A wick that is too large burns too hot and smokes. For an 8 ounce mason jar, a medium cotton wick, usually sold as a 6 inch pre-tabbed wick, is the safe starting point. Buy a wick kit for your jar size, and follow the size guide on the packet.

What you need

  • Soy container wax flakes, about 6 ounces per 8 ounce jar — about $4
  • Clean 8 ounce mason jar, heat-safe — about $1.50, or reuse one you have
  • Pre-tabbed cotton wick, medium, for an 8 ounce jar — about $1
  • Fragrance oil or essential oil, pine, cinnamon, or vanilla — about $3 to $5
  • Wick sticker or a dab of hot glue — free, or about $1
  • A skewer, chopstick, or clothespin, to hold the wick straight — free
  • A double boiler or a heat-safe pouring jug and a saucepan — you probably have these
  • Ribbon and a small pine sprig, to decorate — about $1

Buy soy wax made for containers, not pillar wax, because container wax is softer and sticks to the jar. Use fragrance oil made for candles, not perfume, because perfume can catch fire or smoke. If you use essential oil, use about 1 teaspoon per 6 ounces of wax, and keep the scent simple: pine and cedarwood, cinnamon and orange, or vanilla on its own all work well in winter.

Steps

  1. Fix the wick. Stick the wick tab to the centre of the jar bottom with a wick sticker or a dab of hot glue. Press it firmly, then pull the wick straight up and rest a skewer or chopstick across the top of the jar to hold it in place. Warm the jar with hot tap water first, then dry it, so cold glass does not shock the hot wax.
  2. Melt the wax. Put the soy wax flakes in a double boiler or a pouring jug set in a saucepan of hot water. Melt over low heat, stirring now and then, until the wax is fully clear and smooth. Do not let water get into the wax, and do not heat it past the temperature on the wax packet, usually about 185 degrees Fahrenheit.
  3. Add the scent. Take the wax off the heat. Stir in about 1 ounce of fragrance oil per pound of wax, or about 1 tablespoon per 6 ounces, and stir slowly for two minutes. Stirring for the full two minutes matters, because it helps the scent bind to the wax instead of sitting on top.
  4. Pour slowly. Pour the wax slowly into the jar, leaving about half an inch of space at the top. Pour in a steady stream, not in splashes, and keep the wick centred as you pour. If the wick leans, straighten it with the skewer while the wax is still liquid.
  5. Let it set. Leave the candle on a flat surface at room temperature, without moving it, for at least 4 hours, and ideally overnight. Do not put it in the fridge or freezer to speed it up, because fast cooling causes cracks and sinkholes in the top.
  6. Fix the top if needed. If the top has a small sinkhole, melt a little extra wax and pour a thin layer over the top, or use a heat gun or hair dryer on low to smooth it. This is normal for a first candle, and a smooth top is easy to fix.
  7. Trim and decorate. Trim the wick to about 1/4 inch. Tie a ribbon around the jar, tuck in a small pine sprig, and add a gift tag with the scent and the date you poured it. Remove the ribbon and greenery before you light the candle.
  8. Cure before burning. Wait at least 48 hours before the first burn, so the scent has time to settle into the wax. On the first burn, let the candle burn for 2 to 3 hours, until the melted wax reaches the edge of the jar. This sets the memory of the candle and stops it tunnelling later.

Why homemade candles tunnel or smoke

Tunnelling usually means the wick is too small for the jar, or the first burn was too short. Let the first burn go until the wax melts all the way to the edge, and use the wick size the packet recommends for your jar. Smoking usually means the wick is too long, so trim it to 1/4 inch before every burn. A rough or cracked top usually means the candle cooled too fast, or it was moved while setting. Let it set at room temperature, in a draught-free spot, and do not rush it.

Cost breakdown

Soy wax $4, jar $1.50, wick $1, fragrance oil $3, ribbon $1. Total about $10.50 if you buy everything new, and under $10 if you reuse a jar and ribbon you already have. One bag of wax makes about 3 candles, so the cost per candle drops to about $6 to $7 once you make a batch. A similar soy candle in a shop costs $18 to $28, so a batch of three saves you $30 or more.

Tips to save time and money

Make a batch of three or four at once, because melting wax for one candle takes almost the same time as melting it for four. Use thrifted jars, teacups, and tins as containers, just make sure they are heat-safe and the wick size matches the width. Save leftover candle wax from old candles, melt it down, and pour it into a new jar with a fresh wick. Label each candle with its scent and date, so you remember what you made. Keep a candle journal with the wax, scent, and wick size that worked, so your next batch is even better.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use perfume or food flavouring instead of candle fragrance oil, because it can smoke or catch fire. Do not add dried flowers, pinecones, or cinnamon sticks into the wax, because they can catch fire when the candle burns. Decorate the outside of the jar only. Do not pour hot wax into a cold jar, because the glass can crack. Do not burn a candle for more than 4 hours at a time, and never leave a burning candle unattended, near curtains, or where children and pets can reach it.

Variations to try

Try a teacup candle: pour the same wax into a thrifted teacup and saucer for a gift that looks vintage. Try a layered scent: pour half the wax, let it set, then pour a second scent on top. Try a jam jar candle: tint the wax red, pour it into a small jar, and cover it with gingham fabric and twine, so it looks like a jar of preserves. Try a beeswax version for a honey scent, using beeswax pellets and a braided cotton wick, melted the same way in a double boiler.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a homemade soy candle burn? An 8 ounce soy candle burns for about 30 to 40 hours, if you trim the wick and let each burn reach the edge of the jar. Burn it for 2 to 3 hours at a time for the longest life.

Can I use essential oils instead of fragrance oil? Yes, use about 1 teaspoon per 6 ounces of wax. Pine, cedarwood, cinnamon, orange, and vanilla are the safest winter choices. Some essential oils have a low flash point, so buy oils made for candles and follow the supplier's guide.

Why is my candle top rough or sunk? It cooled too fast, or it was moved while setting. Let it set at room temperature, without moving it, and smooth a rough top with a heat gun or hair dryer on low, or a thin top-up pour of melted wax.

Can kids help make candles? Kids can help decorate the jar and choose the scent, but an adult should handle the hot wax. Melted wax is hot enough to burn skin, so keep children away from the pouring step.
