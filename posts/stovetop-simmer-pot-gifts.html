DIY Stovetop Simmer Pot Gift Bags (Under $5, 10 Minutes)

HBy Hamid Ullah · 28 Nov 2026 · Time: 10 minutes · Cost: under $5 · Skill: Beginner · US supplies

The same gift bags you saw on Pinterest, made in 10 minutes for under $5.

A stovetop simmer pot gift bag is the easiest handmade Christmas gift I know. You put dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks, cloves, and a pine sprig in a clear bag, tie it with twine, and add a gift tag with the instructions. The person who gets it puts the contents in a pot of water, simmers it on the stove, and their whole house smells like Christmas. It takes 10 minutes to make, costs under $5, and you can make a dozen in one afternoon.

This is a good project for a last-minute gift, for a teacher, a neighbour, or a coworker, and for a gift that is useful and consumable, so it does not add clutter. A simmer pot is also a good project for kids, because there is no cooking, no sewing, and no mess. A similar scented gift in a shop often costs $10 to $15, so you save money, and yours smells fresher because you made it with real spices.

How a simmer pot works

A simmer pot works by heating water with spices and fruit in it. The hot water releases the scent from the cinnamon, cloves, oranges, and pine, and the steam carries it around the house. The pot simmers on low heat, never boils hard, and you add water every hour or so to keep it going. One bag lasts for a whole evening, and you can reuse the contents once or twice if you store them in the fridge overnight.

Use dried oranges, not fresh oranges, because dried oranges last longer in the bag and do not go mouldy. Use whole spices, not ground spices, because ground spices make the water cloudy and can burn on the bottom of the pot. Use a small pine sprig, a few cranberries, and a star anise if you have it, for a pretty look and a warm scent. Keep the bag dry until it is used, so the spices stay fresh.

What you need

  • Dried orange slices, 3 to 4 per bag — about $1, or make them at home
  • Cinnamon sticks, 2 to 3 per bag — about $0.50
  • Whole cloves, 1 tablespoon per bag — about $0.50
  • Star anise, 1 per bag — about $0.50, optional
  • Fresh cranberries, a small handful per bag — about $1
  • A small pine or cedar sprig per bag — free from your garden, or about $0.50
  • Clear cellophane bags, 4 by 6 inches — about $0.25 each
  • Twine or ribbon, to tie the bag — about $0.25
  • A gift tag with the instructions, printed or handwritten — free

Buy spices in bulk, because they cost much less per bag that way. A bag of cinnamon sticks that costs $3 makes about 10 gift bags, so each bag costs about 30 cents for cinnamon. Use fresh cranberries, not dried cranberries, because fresh cranberries look prettier in the bag and release a little colour when they simmer.

Steps

  1. Make the dried oranges. If you do not have dried oranges, slice an orange thin, about 1/8 inch thick, and bake the slices at 200 degrees Fahrenheit for 2 to 3 hours, turning them once, until they are dry. Let them cool fully. You can make a batch the day before, and store them in a dry jar.
  2. Layer the bag. Put 3 to 4 dried orange slices in the bottom of a clear cellophane bag, then add 2 to 3 cinnamon sticks, 1 tablespoon of whole cloves, 1 star anise, a small handful of cranberries, and a pine sprig on top. Layering the ingredients so you can see them through the bag makes the gift look pretty.
  3. Tie the bag. Gather the top of the bag, twist it closed, and tie it with twine or ribbon. Tie a simple bow, and trim the ends of the twine so they look neat. Do not tie the bag too tight, or the bag can tear when you open it.
  4. Add the gift tag. Write or print a gift tag with the instructions: "Pour into a pot, add 4 cups of water, simmer on low, add water as needed. Do not leave unattended." Add the scent name, like "Christmas Spice" or "Winter Pine", and the date you made it. Punch a hole in the tag, thread it onto the twine, and tie it on.
  5. Make a batch. Repeat the steps for as many bags as you need. A batch of 10 bags takes about 30 minutes, because you can layer all the bags at once, then tie them all at once. Keep the finished bags in a cool, dry place until you give them away.

Why a simmer pot smells weak or burns

A weak scent usually means the pot was not hot enough, or the spices were old. Simmer the pot on low heat, not on high, and use fresh spices, because old spices lose their scent. A burnt smell usually means the water ran low and the spices stuck to the bottom of the pot. Add water every hour, and never let the pot boil dry. If the water gets cloudy, strain it and start again with fresh water and the same spices, because the spices still have scent left in them.

Cost breakdown

Dried oranges $1, cinnamon $0.50, cloves $0.50, star anise $0.50, cranberries $1, pine sprig free to $0.50, bag $0.25, twine $0.25. Total about $4 to $5 per bag if you buy everything in small amounts, and about $2 to $3 per bag if you buy spices in bulk and make dried oranges at home. A similar scented gift in a shop costs $10 to $15, so you save at least $5 per bag, and a batch of 10 saves you $50 or more.

Tips to save time and money

Buy spices in bulk at a grocery store, because they cost much less than small jars. Make dried oranges in a big batch, because one orange makes about 8 slices, enough for two bags. Use a cookie cutter to cut the gift tags from kraft paper, so they look handmade. Make a batch of 10 bags at once, and keep a few spare for last-minute gifts. Add a small jar of honey or a tea bag to the gift, for a complete cozy gift set.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use ground spices, because they make the water cloudy and can burn. Do not use fresh oranges, because they go mouldy in the bag. Do not tie the bag too tight, because it can tear. Do not forget the gift tag, because the person who gets the bag needs to know how to use it. Do not leave a simmer pot unattended, and never let it boil dry, because the pot can burn.

Variations to try

Try a winter pine version: use pine sprigs, cedar, and rosemary, with no oranges, for a fresh forest scent. Try a cranberry version: use cranberries, oranges, and cinnamon, for a sweet Christmas scent. Try a ginger version: use fresh ginger slices, oranges, and cloves, for a warm spicy scent. Try a mason jar version: put the ingredients in a small mason jar instead of a bag, for a gift that looks like a jar of preserves. Try a bath version: put the same ingredients in a muslin bag, for a bath soak instead of a simmer pot.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a simmer pot last? One bag lasts for an evening, about 4 to 6 hours, if you add water as needed. You can reuse the contents once or twice if you store them in the fridge overnight, but the scent gets weaker each time.

Can I use the simmer pot in a slow cooker? Yes, put the ingredients in a slow cooker with 4 cups of water, and set it to low with the lid off. A slow cooker is safer than a stove, because it will not boil dry as quickly.

Is a simmer pot safe for pets? Some essential oils and spices are not safe for pets, so keep pets out of the room while the pot simmers, and do not use a simmer pot if you have birds, because birds are very sensitive to scents. Check with your vet if you are unsure.

Can kids help make the gift bags? Yes, kids can help layer the bags and tie the twine, and write the gift tags. An adult should handle the dried oranges if they were baked in the oven, and the gift is safe to give to anyone.
